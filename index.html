No. 01 The promise

Everything you enter stays on your device.

Not a marketing line — it is how the thing is built. There is no account to create, no server to sync with, and no third party in the loop. Rootwork has nothing to sell because it never collects anything in the first place, and the only copy of your health history is the one you are holding.

No. 03 Impacts

Free · the thing everyone else charges for

Find out what is actually moving your pain.

Four taps a day — sleep, energy, mood, stress — and Rootwork compares every one of them against your own pain scores. Not averages from a study of strangers. Your last night's sleep, against this morning.

after short nights 6.4

Across 62 days, 0.9 points higher the day after a wired one.

Barometric pressure Nothing obvious

Across 58 days, this doesn't appear to be moving your pain in either direction — which is a real answer, and it is shown rather than quietly dropped.

Measured in pain points, not statistics

"1.8 points higher after a short night" is something you can act on. A correlation coefficient is not. Everything is reported as the difference in your own 0–10 scale.

Lagged where lag makes sense

Sleep, stress and yesterday's activity are compared against the next day. Comparing last night's sleep with last night's pain only measures pain wrecking sleep.

It tells you when it doesn't know

Nothing is reported under fourteen paired days, and the factors that turned out to be nothing are shown as nothing. A confident wrong answer about your own body is worse than no answer at all.

The same table, as a spreadsheet

One row per day, every variable side by side, in plain CSV — literally the sheet the app drew its own conclusions from. If those two ever disagreed, the app would be the thing that's wrong.

No. 04 The Sanctuary

Somewhere to go that isn't about being ill.

A small farm rendered in 3D, sitting inside the app. Clear a plot, plant it, keep bees, watch the weather roll in and the light change with the hour. Logging a day earns a little toward it.

Nothing here punishes you

No timers that run out. No crops that wilt because you were too unwell to open the app. No streak that shames you for a bad fortnight. Come back after a month and it is exactly where you left it — that rule is not a nicety, it is the whole reason this is allowed to exist in a pain app.

It knows what time it is

Real hours of daylight, lanterns at dusk, fireflies after dark. The weather is generated from the date, so everyone running Rootwork gets the same rain on the same day without a server ever being involved.

No. 05 Gardens

An app you can stand to look at on a bad day.

Twelve complete palettes — paper, ink and accents — each with its own drifting particles. Fireflies at midnight, petals in spring, silver light on drifting petals. Tap one and this page changes with it. There are more in the app than are shown here; some you find, and one or two you earn, and naming them on a website would spoil both.

No. 06 PulsePoints

Medicine that's going somewhere.

A small reading room built into the app, for the days the news about being ill is all bad. Written for Rootwork, and published here too — so a piece worth sending to somebody has somewhere to be sent.

In the app it is off by default: PulsePoints makes no network request at all unless you turn it on, and when you do it asks one address for one public file — no account, no cookie, no identifier, no referrer.

No. 09 Questions

The things worth asking first.

Is it really free, or free for now?
Free, and there is no paid tier planned. There is no billing code in the app and no account system to attach one to. If that ever changed it would have to be a different app with a different name.
If there's no server, how do I move to a new phone?
You export a file and import it on the other device. That is deliberately a thing you do rather than a thing that happens — a sync service is a copy of your health history on somebody's computer, and the whole point is that there isn't one.
What happens if I lose my phone?
The data goes with it, unless you exported. This is the real cost of the promise and it would be dishonest to bury it: no server means no recovery. Export occasionally, to somewhere you trust.
Does it ever connect to the internet?
Only if you switch something on. Barometric pressure and the PulsePoints reading room each make a request when enabled, and both are off by default. Nothing is sent — those requests ask for public data and carry no account, cookie, identifier or referrer.
Can it tell me what's causing my pain?
No, and it will not pretend to. It reports associations inside your own log — "your pain runs two points lower after a long night" — and says so in exactly those words. Two things moving together can always be a third thing moving both.
Will my doctor actually look at this?
More likely than at a phone. There is a one-page printable summary built for the ninety seconds they have, and a plain CSV for anyone who wants to do their own arithmetic.
No. 10 Small print

Rootwork is a personal record-keeping tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, or give medical advice. Anything it shows you describes what you recorded — it is not a clinical finding. Always talk to a qualified healthcare professional about your symptoms and treatment.
